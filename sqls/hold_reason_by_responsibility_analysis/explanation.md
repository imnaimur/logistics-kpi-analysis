## Analysis

- Week 2 produced 1,679 holds across the six hubs, and more than half of them came from one place Riverside with 874. No other hub reached 200.

- What sets Riverside apart is who caused them. At the other hubs, at least three in four holds start with the customer  they couldn't be reached, asked for another day or gave an incomplete address. At Riverside it's the reverse. 64% of its holds were agent-side. Late Dispatch alone caused 312, more than the total holds at any other hub, and Not Attempted added another 132. Riverside accounts for 563 of the network's 740 agent-side holds (76%).

- The other five hubs look much alike. Agent-side holds are 19-26% of their total, and each agent-side reason accounts for only 7 to 20 holds over the whole week. Late Dispatch tops the agent-side list at Brookside and Hillcrest; Agent Time Shortage tops it at Greenfield, Westwood and Lakeview. At these small numbers the ranking is a hint, not a pattern, and at Brookside and Hillcrest second place is a tie.

- So Riverside's hold problem is about how its orders get dispatched, not about how many agents it has. Fixing that would cut its holds, free up attempts, and lighten its workload before any agent is added.
